The Evolution of Smart Homes

Introduction

The main idea of the smart home originated from the desire to make homes seem futuristic,
however have now developed to also include tasks such as security and safety,
both of which have become a large reason for the success of the smart home markets across the world.​
Safety includes fire prevention and reaction, automation of the prevention of injury such as falls or slips,
child safety, and more. ​
Security includes locks, doorbells, cameras, alarms, motion sensors,
and other ways to alert residents in the event of burglaries or threats against the home.​
Many of the products bought for these purposes are pre-emptive,
meaning they are bought in preparation for issues that could arise rather than reaction.
​
Historic Developments

The idea took hold in 1963 when fault reporting systems were implemented
in large industrial building units via the conventional electrical instillation. ​
In 1991 this system entered personal homes where heating, cooling,
ventilation and lighting were initiated by Berker, Jung, Gira, Merten and Siemens.
In 2002, the first upscale luxury home was developed with smart systems where basic 
home functions were automated in a more modern manner. ​
By 2015 over a million homes were adapted to smart systems in Europe.
Fast forward to the current day there are numerous inventions implemented into this
broad invention, and developments are being constructed. ​
In this research project we'll be focusing on middle eastern developments;
we'll discuss over the societal and business side of things, the history of developments
and ethical aspects too.​

First Viewpoint Analysis - Societal

Smart homes have become an important part of modern society.
People can control many things in their homes using their smartphones or by giving voice commands.
The development of smart homes has changed the way people live, work, and manage their daily routine. ​
They are also very helpful for elderly people and people with disabilities because
they can manage and certain things at home without needing as much as help from others.​
Overall, smart homes have a positive impact on society but there are still some challenges that
need to be improved.​

Second Viewpoint Analysis - Business

In the business world, smart homes have made a fierce entry in the real estate market. Particularly in 
areas such as Abu Dhabi, Saudi Arabia, Qatar, Singapore and Hong Kong. These are the biggest
profile countries when it comes to the implementation of futuristic lifestyles in accordance to large
scale smart- home developments. Phase 1 of developments generally began amidst the 2000s to the early 2010s;
this was a time when automation was a luxury custom installed niche. Few had access and almost all
systems were individually tailored to high profile clients. Phase 2 commenced from 2012-2019;
this marked a new chapter where cheap installments such as LED bulb, home surveillance and voice
assistance were introduced into more average households. 
A large debate occurs in the business world rooting from the discussion whether incooperating smart products
and systems in homes qualify as a "smart house" altogether. The aggrivation from this debate stems from
the confussion regarding what statistics are technically correct. Secondary data reports 45% of US
internet households own at least one core smart home device. 
Phase 3 introduces Ecosystems such as Zigabee into the market. Big heads in business such as Amazon,
Apple and Google begin to place their foot in the door with the introduction of Ecosystem solutions.
This era revolutionalised developers approach to focus more on reliability and convenience between 
different appliances. 
Phase 4 is the current phase. AI driven homes have entered the market. The diection this follows is such:
homes are aimed to work without the need to be 'asked'. Fully functional homes are the goal here,
this includes traits such as presence detection, energy optimisation and predictive climate control
from learned routines of clients. 
The customers who generally invest in smart home functionality are high profile clients. They value 
convenience, status and luxury. A main attraction point for them is the satrong energy management aspect,
in hotter regions more cooling is required. A big concern from this audience is brand trust, privacy and 
consistent mainetnance which is currently lacking after installments. 
The biggest competitors in the game are companies such as Creston, Control4, Savant and KNX, particularly
KNX who has had their place on the table for longer than others.

Ethical Reflections and Improvements

Ethical Reflections ​

Privacy ​
Smart home devices collect personal information about users, including their daily routines,
activities and personal preferences. This creates privacy concerns, as users may not know 
exactly what information is being collected, stored or shared with other companies.​
Network Security​
Smart home devices are connected to the internet, which can make them vulnerable to
hackers and cyberattacks. If someone gains unauthorized access to these devices, they
could most likely access the private information or take control of certain connected system.​
Physical safety​
Smart home technology can create a physical safety risks if the device  are hacked,
damaged or malfunction. For example, problems with smart locks, security cameras or
heating systems could potentially put houseowners and their families in danger.

 
Ethical Improvements of  Smart Homes (hi)



 

Although smart homes provide a range of benefits, there is still a lot of room for advancements in their technology regarding security, customer privacy, accessibility, and much more.
With the rapid evolution of smart homes- their future advancements should not only focus on technological upgrade, 
but as well as the ethical responsibilities builders owe to the customers in order to ensure inclusivity, sustainability and safer user experiences. 
By bringing these concerns forward, it provides a better chance at getting users the type of experiences they deserve. 

 
Security Vulnerabilities 

With the rising cybersecurity attacks, smart homes have become more vulnerable to these threats. 
Security attacks may take place remotely or locally with the most prominent security risks being related to weak device and user encryption schemes.
Manufacturers need to implement ways of reducing this risk, especially through building stronger encryption, authentication factors, and keeping security systems regularly updated automatically. 
Keeping the systems updated regularly should be able to support older devices so that they do not become prone to attacks. 

 
Privacy and Data Handling Concerns 

Devices in smart homes collect heavy amounts of personal data such as daily routines, household activities, and sometimes conversations. 
These can lead to users having shaky trust as they are usually unaware of who is handling and receiving their most private information behind the scenes and/or get vague and fine printed information on it.
Manufacturers should prioritize complete transparency and allow the users to have greater control over how their data is being handled. 
With the artificial intelligence integration increasing, users now rely on technology more than they did before. 

 Users should be able to monitor their data movement and be able to override automatic systems without losing important functionality. 
AI-controlled systems should be used to support human decision-making and work alongside humans to make daily life much simpler and not eliminate control from them. 
Most importantly, the exploitation of customer data for commercial reasons, without getting customer consent first, should be foremost. 
Manufacturers should implement frameworks that provide clear consent options and where users are allowed to terminate thier personal data from any set up system at any point. 

 
Inclusivity 

There's been many times where emerging technologies have left people from less fortunate minority groups. 
This creates an imbalance for users to keep up with companies and gradually cut ties with the manufacturer. 
Builders should put effort in making the technologies more flexible and available for everyone's access. 
Systems such as voice assistance and accessible multi-dial alerts could be features for future improvements. 

 

In the long run, the evolution of smart homes should keep a balance between manufacturer's ethical responsibility and future innovations and improvements. 
Users should be able to rely on their technology without having to worry about their safety, living comfortably in their homes, and their freedom. 

 

 

Conclusion 

Smart homes have made everyday life easier, safer and more convenient,
especially for families and elderly people. However, privacy, security and high
costs remain challenges that need to be improved for the future.  ​
