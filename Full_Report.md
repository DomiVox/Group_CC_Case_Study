## The Evolution of Smart Homes

# Introduction

We have chosen our research point to be smart homes. Smart homes are a modern implementation in the real estate world and they are a new revolutionary development to transform modern day home life. ​
​Smart homes are defined as homes that have additions to them that can improve quality of life dramatically, mainly by being connected to the Internet or interacting with other devices to manage day to day running of a home. ​
​When the idea was first conceptualised it was related to very basic tasks however it has developed into what it is known for today, large amounts of internet connected devices that intertwine to collect data, actively monitor systems, change environment conditions or generally allow for easier home life. ​

# Introduction (2)

The main idea of the smart home originated from the desire to make homes seem futuristic, however have now developed to also include tasks such as security and safety, both of which have become a large reason for the success of the smart home markets across the world.​
​Safety includes fire prevention and reaction, automation of the prevention of injury such as falls or slips, child safety, and more. ​
​Security includes locks, doorbells, cameras, alarms, motion sensors, and other ways to alert residents in the event of burglaries or threats against the home.​
​Most of the products bought for these purposes are pre-emptive, meaning they are bought in preparation for issues that could arise rather than reaction.​


# Historic Developments

​The idea took hold in 1963 when fault reporting systems were implemented in large industrial building units via conventional electrical instillation, although these systems did not have much interconnectedness and primarily relied on switches and basic alarms, they laid the groundwork for the future of smart homes.​
In the 1970's buildings started to have dedicated automatic fire alarm systems where fire alarms could be automatically be triggered by smoke alarms or the activation of sprinkler systems, in addition to this ''Event driven'' reactions could start to take place due to the advent of microprocessors which allowed systems to react if there was an issue; for example closing air supply to a room if a fire is detected.​
In 1991 this system entered personal homes where automated heating, cooling, ventilation and lighting systems were standardised by Berker, Jung, Gira, Merten and Siemens, the benefits of standardising the systems were that devices could interconnect and ''Talk'' to each other no matter the brand. This was especially important for the personal home over businesses who may have had less resources to troubleshoot.​

In 2002, the first luxury homes were built and developed with smart systems where many of the home functions we see today were incorporated, this includes automatic opening and closing of shades, lamps and lights being able to be turned off and on and even dimmed, air conditioning, heating and more. This took us a step further as many of these systems could now be selected to turn on or off at certain times, react to the presence of people in rooms or simply add a technological flair to the houses to add to their ''luxury''. ​
​By 2005 Screens and panels that could control all these systems centrally had been successfully implemented in some capacity.  Centrally in this sense refers to a single point of the house can control all systems, for example the lights in the bedroom could be controlled from downstairs or the air conditioning in the garage could be operated from the centre of the house.

Ethical Reflections and Improvements

Ethical Reflections ​

Privacy ​

Privacy in smart homes is an ethical concern because connected devices can collect a lot of information about people's routines inside their homes. Cameras, smart Tv's and thermostat can reveal when residents are at home and who visits them and how each of the rooms are being used. Even though these data can make devices more convenient, it may also expose peoples details that people never even wanted to share​. 

Many users accept the terms and conditions without even fully reading the whole thing and without even understanding what is been collected from them and how long they are going to store this data or who else can access these data ​

Privacy is also a shared issue when it comes to smart homes because the people who lives in the house, guests, and workers may be recorded without even choosing to using the device ​

Companies need to explain data practices clearly and only collect what they needed to and give controls to the users to delete information or disable the recording. Smart home should provide the residences convenience without making people being watched in their own household.​

Network Security​

Network security is important because the smart home devices are always on the internet and talking to each other. If your home network isn't secure then a hacker or a piece of malware could get in and access your personal data or even take over your devices. It's a bit of a scary thought really as you're in your own home and you'd expect to be safe.​

Protecting a smart home shouldn't in any way require a degree in computer science. The companies that make the devices have a job to build security in them with regular software updates, clear setup instructions, and strong default security. The home owner/resident also has a part to play they could secure their accounts with  strong passwords, keeping the devices updated and securing the Wi-Fi.​

Staying safe online shouldn't be entirely on the user, manufacturers should be upfront about the risks and should continue to provide security updates to their products for a reasonable period of time. Network safety is about much more than just stopping cyber attacks. It's about protecting people's privacy, their security and their peace of mind. Smart home technology is supposed to make life easier, not to make peoples life harder.​


Physical safety
​
Smart home devices can be a good thing they can provide safety and security but they can also be an issue if they fail. A smart lock can malfunction and leave you locked out of your own house. A faulty smoke detector could fail to give you an alarm in the case of a fire. Even a fairly simple piece of kit can be a hazard if it's not installed properly or if it's not maintained.​

As they're going to be using the devices every day it makes perfect sense to have a backup. A physical key and/or knowing how to use the manual override would be useful in the event of the device going dead or losing its internet connection. It's absolutely critical that everyone in the house knows how to operate the key equipment, especially in an emergency.​

Companies have to test their products properly, they have to be absolutely clear about any risks and they have to get rid of any safety issues as fast as possible. That's the whole point of smart technology isn't it? To make people safer. Not to remove the need for any safety whatsoever and then leave people totally exposed to the device if it fails.​

 
Ethical Improvements of  Smart Homes 

Although smart homes provide a range of benefits, there is still a lot of room for advancements in their technology regarding security, customer privacy, accessibility, and much more.
With the rapid evolution of smart homes- their future advancements should not only focus on technological upgrade, 
but as well as the ethical responsibilities builders owe to the customers in order to ensure inclusivity, sustainability and safer user experiences. 
By bringing these concerns forward, it provides a better chance at getting users the type of experiences they deserve. 

 
Security Vulnerabilities 

With the rising cybersecurity attacks, smart homes have become more vulnerable to these threats. 
Security attacks may take place remotely or locally with the most prominent security risks being related to weak device and user encryption schemes.
Manufacturers need to implement ways of reducing this risk, especially through building stronger encryption, authentication factors, and keeping security systems regularly updated automatically. 
Keeping the systems updated regularly should be able to support older devices so that they do not become prone to attacks. 

 
Privacy and Data Handling Concerns 

Devices in smart homes collect heavy amounts of personal data such as daily routines, household activities, and sometimes conversations. 
These can lead to users having shaky trust as they are usually unaware of who is handling and receiving their most private information behind the scenes and/or get vague and fine printed information on it.
Manufacturers should prioritize complete transparency and allow the users to have greater control over how their data is being handled. 
With the artificial intelligence integration increasing, users now rely on technology more than they did before. 

 Users should be able to monitor their data movement and be able to override automatic systems without losing important functionality. 
AI-controlled systems should be used to support human decision-making and work alongside humans to make daily life much simpler and not eliminate control from them. 
Most importantly, the exploitation of customer data for commercial reasons, without getting customer consent first, should be foremost. 
Manufacturers should implement frameworks that provide clear consent options and where users are allowed to terminate thier personal data from any set up system at any point. 

 
Inclusivity 

There's been many times where emerging technologies have left people from less fortunate minority groups. 
This creates an imbalance for users to keep up with companies and gradually cut ties with the manufacturer. 
Builders should put effort in making the technologies more flexible and available for everyone's access. 
Systems such as voice assistance and accessible multi-dial alerts could be features for future improvements. 

 

In the long run, the evolution of smart homes should keep a balance between manufacturer's ethical responsibility and future innovations and improvements. 
Users should be able to rely on their technology without having to worry about their safety, living comfortably in their homes, and their freedom. 

 

 

Conclusion 

Smart homes have made everyday life easier, safer and more convenient,
especially for families and elderly people. However, privacy, security and high
costs remain challenges that need to be improved for the future.

It would be a chance for individuals throughout all demographics to benefit from the technology. 
For future improvements manufacturers are encouraged to prioritize human needs along the technological advancements as well. This will ensure a sustainable and inclusive system which will be beneficial long term- as the manufacturers and consumers will have a standing inclusive system. 
Smart homes improve the quality of people's lives and if the leaders of this market actively work on advancing and stabilizing the systems that are already set in place, not only will consumers be keen to buy/rent them; but the smart home market will also flourish and continue thriving as more people will be in demand for them. ​
